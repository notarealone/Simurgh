# Self-RAG: Learning to Retrieve, Generate, and Critique through Self-Reflection

- **Authors:** Akari Asai, Zeqiu Wu, Yizhong Wang, Avirup Sil, Hannaneh Hajishirzi
- **Year:** 2024
- **Venue:** ICLR 2024
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11511

---

## Summary

Self-RAG trains a single language model to interleave retrieval and generation adaptively: the model emits special **reflection tokens** to decide when to retrieve, to assess the relevance of retrieved passages, and to critique its own output for faithfulness and overall quality. Because critique is internalized rather than outsourced, the model can be steered at inference time — different token weights shift the trade-off between factuality and fluency — without retraining. 7B and 13B Self-RAG models outperform same-size baselines and match or exceed larger models (GPT-3.5, Llama2-chat 70B) on open-domain QA, reasoning, and fact-verification benchmarks.

## Key Contributions

- Introduces **reflection tokens** (Retrieve, IsREL, IsSUP, IsUSE) as a lightweight mechanism to make retrieval decisions and quality critiques part of the model's vocabulary, enabling on-the-fly control.
- Trains on a dataset of (instruction, passage, output, critique) tuples generated by a stronger teacher model, framing self-critique as supervised fine-tuning rather than RLHF.
- Demonstrates **inference-time controllability**: by adjusting reflection-token weights a single model can optimize for citation accuracy, factuality, or fluency depending on task demands.
- Shows that selective, on-demand retrieval outperforms always-retrieve and no-retrieve strategies across diverse tasks.
- Achieves competitive results at 7B/13B scale, supporting deployment under low-compute constraints.

## Relevance to This Thesis

Self-RAG's reflection-token framework is a template for inserting learnable decision points into the RAG pipeline — in Simurgh this idea transfers to the query rewriter deciding *whether* and *how* to rewrite a query for a given persona. The teacher-generated critique dataset mirrors Simurgh's LLM-as-simulator approach: preference pairs and quality signals are produced by a stronger model and used to train a smaller one via supervised or preference learning (DPO). The inference-time controllability principle also maps onto persona-conditioned generation, where different learner profiles could shift the output style without retraining.

## Notes

- Self-RAG relies on a capable teacher (GPT-4 class) for annotation; Simurgh similarly depends on a strong judge LLM, so the same caveat applies: judge quality caps trainable-policy quality.
- The model is trained end-to-end with reflection tokens, which requires modifying the training pipeline; for Simurgh's DPO-only path, the relevant takeaway is the *data-generation recipe* (teacher critique → student training) rather than the full architecture.
- Evaluated on English; no multilingual or Persian experiments. Reflection tokens would need the base model to handle Persian fluently, which limits direct reuse but not the conceptual transfer.
- Open question: can a LoRA adapter over a small Persian-capable LLM reproduce selective retrieval behavior with DPO instead of the supervised reflection-token training used here?
