## Stage 1 retriever — after the `reader_kd` negative result

Context and the full evidence table live in [methodology](methodology.md), "Why
`reader_kd` was retired as the primary arm". Three items stay open.

- [ ] **Reader-in-the-loop teacher — the principled fix, deferred.** Our teacher is a
  subjective LLM-judge rating on the **nano-era** labels (single-label reliability ≈ 0.17);
  LaMP's is a *measured* reader outcome, `Eval(y, M(φp(x,[d])))`, deterministic under greedy
  decoding. Every question in the current bank carries both gold fields: **618/618** have
  an `answer` and **618/618** have an `explanation`. The all-question type mix is
  `short_answer` 259, `fill_blank` 130, `true_false` 85, `matching` 79, `mcq` 56,
  `essay` 9, with **350** closed-form questions. The 524-question train+val qid pool
  contains `short_answer` 224, `fill_blank` 110, `true_false` 69, `matching` 66,
  `mcq` 48, and `essay` 7, with **293** closed-form questions. Of the 618 gold records,
  487 (78.8%, about 79%) are AI-generated and their answers/explanations are
  model-authored, not human-verified, so the signal is computable on **100% of the data**:
  exact match for the closed-form questions, ROUGE-1 for `short_answer`, at
  `temperature: 0`. Same call budget as today's judge
  (20 reader calls per group instead of 20 judge calls). **The adaptation that matters:**
  scoring against a single gold `y` is persona-*independent*, so a naive port would delete
  the personalization signal entirely — LaMP gets persona-dependence from its per-user
  corpus, which we do not have. The fix is to condition the **reader** on the persona,
  `Eval(y, M(φp(x,[d],persona)))`: a chunk that lets a crammer-prompted reader answer
  correctly differs from one that works for a scholar-prompted reader, and the score stays
  deterministic. This also decomposes for free — run the reader with and without the
  persona and the difference *is* the persona signal, measurable before committing a
  training run to it.
- [x] **Paired significance testing — done.** `benchmarks/compare_runs.py` consumes the
  per-query vectors from `training_log.json`: bootstrap CI on the paired mean difference,
  sign-flip permutation p-value, Holm-corrected across the metric family, overall and per
  persona. It compares epoch-vs-baseline within one log, arm-vs-arm across two, and
  matched-vs-swapped persona within one (`--swap`). Cross-arm runs get a baseline-parity
  check: two arms on the same val set must produce identical epoch-0 vectors, since the
  adapter is the identity there. (A simpler epoch-vs-baseline bootstrap already lived in
  `train_ropg_kd.ipynb`; the offline tool adds arm-vs-arm, p-values, per-persona
  breakdowns, and the parity guard, and runs without a Kaggle session.) The Luna-era
  validation numbers that made this urgent: per persona *n* = 92, giving SE ≈ 0.043 on
  Hit@5 and ≈ 0.027 on nDCG@5 — so the *baseline* per-persona nDCG@5 spread
  (0.5368 / 0.5591) is under one standard error before any gradient is taken. "One
  persona trains better than the others" is not distinguishable from noise.

- [ ] **Persona-contrastive negatives — the personalisation lever, deferred.**
  **Trigger: implement this if runs C and A plateau well below the persona-blind ceiling
  *and* the persona-swap control shows the encoder ignoring the `Instruct:` prefix.** Not
  before — at **nano-era nDCG@5 0.522** the dominant deficit is generic retrieval, not
  persona-blindness.
  - *The gap.* `mnrl_loss` scores each query against **its own** negatives only
    (`torch.bmm` in `src/rl/ropg_kd.py`) — there are no cross-query in-batch negatives.
    Those negatives come from the tail of the judged list, i.e. plainly irrelevant
    documents. Nothing in the objective ever asks why *this* chunk beats the one a
    different persona wanted, so ignoring the persona prefix is a perfectly good way to
    lower the loss. The model can win the whole headline table on generic Persian
    retrieval and personalise nothing.
  - *The ceiling it targets.* The nano-era val teacher scores give a perfect
    **persona-blind** ranker nDCG@5 0.8781 and a perfect persona-matched one 1.0000.
    That 0.122 is the nano-era Stage-1 personalisation headroom. Under the Luna-era
    labels, the corresponding ceiling is 0.9573, the matched ceiling is 1.0000, and
    headroom is 0.0427. Ranking with the wrong persona costs 0.3369 nDCG@5 in the
    nano era and 0.1363 nDCG@5 in the Luna era — still non-trivial, though roughly
    2.5× smaller — so the personas disagree at the Luna magnitude and the headroom is
    real signal, not label noise.
  - *The fix is data-level, not architectural.* Because negatives are per-row, this needs
    no sampler, collator, or loss change: append the other two personas' positives to
    each row's `negatives` inside `derive_triplets`, behind its own config flag and in a
    separate field so the arm stays independently ablatable. The existing `neg_mask`
    already handles the ragged rows that result.
  - *Measured on `data/ropg_kd/train_triplets.jsonl`.* The current dataset has 432
    questions × 3 personas × 2 others = 2592 candidate additions. The percentages
    below were measured on the earlier 431-question figure and are therefore approximate:
    **64.7%** are new, genuine persona-contrastive negatives; **5.9%** already sit in
    the row's hard negatives (no-op); **29.5%** are identical to the row's own positive
    and must be dropped by an equality check. 83.8% of questions have ≥2 distinct
    positives across personas and 44.1% have all three distinct, which is why the signal
    exists at all.
- [ ] **The metric shares the training labels' noise.** nDCG is graded against the same
  teacher scores that train the encoder, so the Luna-era untrained baseline's 0.5861 may
  already be near this measuring stick's ceiling — we cannot presently separate "training
  failed" from "the metric is noise". A small human-labelled eval set (~100 groups, held
  out, never trained on) is the only clean way out, and it is what the existing
  human-validation TODO under **Reward Signal** should be scoped to cover.

**Decided**

- *Trained components.* Two components are trained, in order: (1) the **retriever** (Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B + LoRA, ROPG-KD) and (2) the **query rewriter** (Qwen3-4B + LoRA, DPO). The generator remains frozen (a light-but-big API model). Training the retriever first ensures DPO preference pairs are built against a stable retriever.
- *Retriever.* Trained with **ROPG-KD** — now a core component, not a stretch. Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B fine-tuned offline via knowledge distillation from LLM judge scores (direct document scoring). ROPG-RL (online) remains out of scope. See Stage 1 in [methodology](methodology.md).
- *Generator personalization.* Persona-blind vs persona-aware is an experimental axis, not a fixed choice (the rewriter-redundancy question).
- *Retriever starting point.* Phase 0 uses lexical BM25 (SQLite FTS5). Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B is validated as the dense base (Rung 1) before ROPG-KD fine-tuning (Rung 3).
- *Rewriter policy model.* Qwen3-4B + LoRA.
- *ROPG-KD teacher signal.* Direct document scoring chosen over generation-mediated scoring. Rationale: direct scoring requires one judge call per `(query, persona, document)` triple vs. one generation + one judge call for generation-mediated, and avoids generation noise obscuring the document's intrinsic utility. The generation-mediated option is noted in [methodology](methodology.md) for completeness.
- *Data-generation judges.* **gpt-5.6-luna** (`reasoning_effort: none`) is the current
  ROPG-KD teacher. The design called for `gpt-5.4-mini`; the pipeline actually ran
  `gpt-5.4-nano` (noted 2026-08-20), then upgraded to Luna in commit `ff90cf6`
  (2026-08-28) during the regeneration already required by the complete-question and
  retry fixes. The upgrade used no extra pass and a 16-token completion budget; the nano
  labels are archived at `data/ropg_kd/old_v2/`. Luna also judges DPO rewrites, whose
  candidates are sampled from `grok-4-1-fast` (the local Gemma-4-E4B simulator was
  dropped for insufficient rewrite quality); Qwen3-4B + LoRA is the policy being trained
  on those pairs. Rationale: price and generation time — the ROPG teacher scores 20
  chunks per `(query, persona)` and the DPO judge rates one short rewrite per call. Per
  the guard-the-judge rule, the *evaluation* judge (still open above) must not reuse
  these models or prompts.
- *Generator access.* One OpenAI-compatible client serves both API models (OpenAI, Google AI Studio) and local servers (LMStudio, llama.cpp); the endpoint and key come from the `OPENAI_BASE_URL` and `OPENAI_API_KEY` environment variables, the model from config.
- *Checkpoint selection (ROPG-KD).* Best checkpoint = highest overall val Recall@K across personas (ties broken by lower val KD loss, then higher MRR) — not train/val KD loss alone. This guards against an encoder that minimises KL while degrading real retrieval (the reward-hacking check from Stage 1). Relevance for Recall@K/MRR is defined as the teacher's top-3 docs per `(query, persona)` group; alternatives considered are recorded in [methodology](methodology.md).
- *ROPG-KD encoder parity.* The training encoder must match the serving encoder exactly: Qwen3-Embedding native **last-token** pooling, documents encoded bare, queries wrapped as `Instruct: <profile>\nQuery: <text>`. Rationale: an earlier revision mean-pooled over a raw `<profile>\n\n<query>` string while `src/rag/embedder.py` served with last-token pooling, so the adapter was trained through one pooling head and read out through another — a silent loss with no error and a normal-looking loss curve. `benchmarks/check_encoder_parity.py` gates this at cosine ≥ 0.999 and must pass before any ROPG-KD result is reported. The same applies to `max_seq_length`, which must match between training and index build or the same chunk gets two different embeddings.
- *Embedder sequence length.* `embedder.max_seq_length: 2048` everywhere the encoder runs — ROPG-KD training, the parity gate, and every index build in `configs/phase*.yaml`. Rationale: at 512 the retriever saw only the head of a long chunk, so its embedding described the introduction rather than the passage the judge scored. The corpus p95 chunk is ~1.5k tokens, so 2048 fits all but the longest few whole; Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B supports far more, but activation memory scales with length and Kaggle's T4s have 16 GB. Settling the memory/throughput trade-off took two corrections worth recording: `training.doc_micro_batch` was first dropped 8 -> 2 on the mistaken belief that it caps peak activation memory. It does not — all micro-forwards stay in one autograd graph until `backward()`, so *retained* activations scale with tokens per step and only `batch_size` controls them; `doc_micro_batch` caps just the transient per-forward working set. The low value cost several-fold throughput by starving the T4 on 2-sequence launches. Final values: `batch_size: 2`, `doc_micro_batch: 10`, `eval.eval_batch_size: 32` (raised from 8 once it was clear eval encoding is `no_grad`, so it shares no constraint with training `batch_size`; it covers ~5% of an epoch's forwards on rank 0 alone and is not a throughput lever either way). All three are throughput/memory knobs that leave the optimizer step unchanged, whose effective batch is still `batch_size x world_size`. Raising the sequence length again means lowering `batch_size` — not `doc_micro_batch` — and rebuilding the index; see the parity note above.
- *ROPG-KD padding waste.* Measured with the real tokenizer over `data/ropg_kd/train.jsonl`: the 171 unique chunks have a median of **167 tokens** (p90 1487) and only 10 reach the 2048 cap, but `collate_scored` pads to the longest sequence in the whole batch, which is 2048 in the *median* batch. Roughly **65% of every FLOP was padding**. `encode_chunked` now sorts documents by true length before micro-batching and trims each micro-batch to its own width, restoring the caller's row order via an inverse permutation (both call sites `view(B, D, -1)` the result against `gold_scores`, so a lost permutation would silently misalign documents). The change is exact, not approximate: padding is already masked out of attention, `last_token_pool` reads the final token which left padding never touches, and RoPE encodes relative position so shifting every token left is invisible. Measured speedup at `batch_size: 2`: 1.68x at `doc_micro_batch: 20`, **2.21x at 10**, 2.54x at 5 — 10 keeps launches large enough not to starve the GPU. This inverts the earlier lesson: a smaller `doc_micro_batch` now buys real compute, not just a smaller transient buffer. No index rebuild or parity re-check is implied, since `max_seq_length` and the embeddings themselves are unchanged.
- *ROPG-KD gradient checkpointing is not a tunable.* Disabling it to gain throughput OOM'd at `batch_size: 2 / doc_micro_batch: 20` and again at `1 / 5`. Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B is 28 layers at hidden 1024, so retained activations run ~30 KB per token per layer in fp16 (~0.85 MB per token across the stack); one `batch_size: 1` step covers 21 sequences × 2048 padded tokens ≈ **36 GB**, against ~2.5 GB when only each block's input is stored. No `batch_size`/`doc_micro_batch` pair fits in a T4's 16 GB without it. Its cost is one extra forward (~33%), so removing it could never have returned more than 1.33x — well under the 2.2x the padding fix gives. `doc_micro_batch: 5` could not have rescued it either, since micro-forwards share one graph until `backward()`.
- *ROPG-KD retrieval metrics — nDCG@1 + Recall@5, graded by teacher score.* The nano-era
  measurements on `data/ropg_kd/old_v2/val.jsonl` were mean scores by rank
  0.757 / 0.569 / 0.447 / 0.372 / 0.323, a rank-3-to-rank-4 gap under 0.05 in 53.3%
  of groups, and threshold emptying of 79/276 groups at 0.7 (125/276 at 0.8). The
  current Luna-era measurements are mean scores 0.799 / 0.372 / 0.176 / 0.108 / 0.072,
  a rank-3-to-rank-4 gap under 0.05 in 61.6% of groups, and threshold emptying of
  58/276 groups at 0.7 (63/276 at 0.8). The code selects checkpoints on Recall@K;
  [experiment-design](experiment-design.md) resolves the headline metric in favour of
  that implementation.
- *ROPG-KD epoch-0 baseline and paired significance testing.* Validation now runs once before training. This is not just convenience: PEFT zero-initializes `lora_B` and `eval_epoch` calls `model.eval()`, so a fresh adapter is **exactly the identity** and epoch-0 metrics *are* the frozen Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B — Rung 1 of the baseline ladder, measured through the same val set, relevance definition and code path as Rung 3, rather than reconstructed by hand from `notebooks/eval_ablation.ipynb`. It also exercises the whole eval path (corpus encoding, DDP all-reduce, rank-0 barrier, checkpoint dir) in minutes instead of after a two-hour epoch. Epoch 0 is **excluded from checkpoint selection** — an identity adapter winning would silently make every downstream Rung 3 number equal Rung 1 — with an explicit end-of-run `baseline -> best` comparison and a `WARNING` when nothing beat it, so a negative result stays loud. `evaluate_retrieval` also returns per-query vectors, persisted to `training_log.json`, because a mean supports a marginal CI but never a **paired** one: with 276 queries a single epoch's 95% CI is ≈ ±0.04, so epoch 1's 0.3406 and epoch 2's 0.3925 have overlapping marginal intervals and read as non-significant — while a paired bootstrap (same queries both times, per-query correlation ≈ 0.77) is ~35% tighter and puts that +0.052 near [+0.025, +0.079], excluding zero. Same data, opposite conclusion; overlapping error bars are a bad proxy for a paired test. One invariant the analysis rests on: pairing is **by position**, valid because group order and the skip decision depend only on the data, never on the model.
- *ROPG-KD LR scheduler warning.* `UserWarning: Detected call of lr_scheduler.step() before optimizer.step()` was **not** an ordering bug — `scaler.step(optimizer)` already precedes `scheduler.step()`. `GradScaler` starts at scale 65536, the first fp16 backward overflows, `scaler.step()` therefore skips the update and halves the scale, and PyTorch sees a scheduler step with no optimizer step behind it. Normal calibration; it fired once per rank at step 1. The only real consequence was the LR schedule advancing for updates that never happened (one or two steps out of 969). `train_epoch` now gates `scheduler.step()` on `scaler.get_scale() >= scale_before`, the standard idiom for detecting a skipped step.
- *ROPG-KD attention implementation.* `attn_implementation="sdpa"` is passed explicitly in `QwenEmbeddingModel`. Rationale: a 2×T4 run OOM'd on rank 1 partway through epoch 3 — after two clean epochs — trying to allocate **2.50 GiB** inside `backward()`. That figure is exactly `10 rows × 16 heads × 2048² × 4 bytes`, i.e. the attention score matrix for one `doc_micro_batch` at full width, upcast to fp32 by eager attention's `softmax(..., dtype=torch.float32)`, and allocated a second time during gradient checkpointing's backward recompute. The lesson worth keeping is *why it failed late*: peak memory scales with `rows × width²` and width is set by whichever documents the shuffle put in that batch, so with only 10 of 171 corpus chunks at the 2048 cap, most batches never approach the peak — `set_epoch` reshuffles and epoch 3 simply drew a bad one. Epochs 1–2 were lucky, not safe. This also bounds what the padding fix above could do: it cut the *linear* retained term ~2.9×, but the quadratic transient is untouchable by trimming, because those documents are genuinely 2048 tokens long. SDPA either drops the matrix entirely (mem-efficient kernel; flash needs sm_80, so not on a T4) or falls back to math, which materializes it in fp16 with no fp32 softmax — half the memory. Both outcomes clear this OOM, which is why no `try`/`except` softens it: a silent fall back to eager would resurface as the same OOM with nothing in the log. `train()` now logs peak allocated/reserved memory per epoch **on every rank**, so a flat series (worst-batch property) can be told apart from a climbing one (a real leak) without guessing. Contingency if SDPA lands on the math fallback and ~1.3 GiB of headroom proves thin: `doc_micro_batch` 10 → 5.
- *DDP launcher.* Multi-GPU ROPG-KD is launched with `torchrun` (a plain `!torchrun` cell, as in the supervisor's notebooks); the `torch.multiprocessing.spawn` path was removed. Rationale: the first 2x T4 attempt under spawn hung with no progress and no traceback, while torchrun surfaced a clean per-rank traceback on its first run — and torchrun is what the ecosystem and the supervisor's scripts assume. The likely cause of the hang was NCCL rendezvous, so the launch sets `NCCL_SHM_DISABLE=1` / `NCCL_P2P_DISABLE=1` (Kaggle's small `/dev/shm` and the absence of NVLink can stall NCCL's default transports; the bandwidth cost is irrelevant for ~5M LoRA gradients) and `init_process_group` uses a 10-minute timeout instead of NCCL's ~30-minute default, so a stall raises rather than hangs. `--standalone` picks a fresh rendezvous port per run so a crashed attempt cannot block the next. Consequence: `CFG` must be written to YAML before launch, since a fresh interpreter cannot be handed a Python dict — which also leaves each run reproducible from its config artifact. **Honest caveat:** the original hang was worked around, not root-caused; if DDP stalls again, start with `NCCL_DEBUG=INFO`.
- *ROPG-KD softmax temperatures.* student 0.05, teacher 0.2 (previously 1.0 for both). Rationale: student scores are cosine similarities in [-1, 1] and teacher scores lie in [0, 1]; at temperature 1.0 a softmax over 20 candidates is near-uniform on both sides, so the KD gradient carries almost no signal. Still to be confirmed by ablation — a reasoned default, not a measured optimum. See Stage 1 in [methodology](methodology.md).

The module is kept only pending cleanup and should not be treated as the source of truth for the ROPG-KD scoring pipeline.

- *ROPG-KD `max_negatives` was never wired.* `derive_triplets` read `cfg["max_negatives"]` from the **top level** of `configs/datagen_ropg.yaml`, where no such key has ever existed, so every triplet file was built with the default 4 regardless of config. Now read from `triplets.max_negatives` and raised to 8. Current 8-negative files intentionally use ranks 13-20 from the judged top 20, in rank order, and this behavior is frozen for Runs A-C despite the historical `hard_neg` name. Correcting that selection requires a new arm or rerunning earlier arms. The mean positive−negative gap only falls 0.711 → 0.686 while the MNRL softmax gains twice the contrastive terms. Two related fixes shipped with it: negatives are sliced with the positive excluded (at `max_negatives >= len(docs)` the window reached back far enough to emit the positive as its own negative), and `mnrl_loss` now honours the `neg_mask` that `collate_triplets` had always produced but nothing consumed. The mask was latent while every group carried exactly 4 negatives; `min_negative_margin` makes row lengths ragged, at which point padded empty-string slots would otherwise compete with the positive as genuine negatives.
- *Open: alternative ROPG-KD negative selection.* Instead of the current ranks 13-20, select the highest-scored non-positive judged documents, ranks 2-9. This is the genuinely harder contrastive task and may provide stronger gradients, but rank 2 and nearby documents have the highest false-negative risk because teacher gaps are noisy. `min_negative_margin` would remove unsafe near-ties and make rows ragged. Current behavior is `sorted_docs[1:][-max_negatives:]`; the harder alternative is `sorted_docs[1:1 + max_negatives]`. This must be a separately named data/run arm, or comparison arms must be rerun, because changing it only for Run C would destroy filtering attribution.
- *DPO datagen recipe — three temperatures, Luna judge, real retries.* Five changes to `configs/datagen_dpo.yaml` and `src/data/gen_dpo_data.py`, mirrored into `notebooks/gen_dpo_data.ipynb` (which carries its own standalone copies of the same code). (1) Rewriter temperatures 4 → 3, `[0.2, 0.5, 0.9]`: four values bought density inside the range, not width, and each one costs a rewrite call plus a judge call per persona. (2) Judge `gpt-5.4-nano` → **gpt-5.6-luna** at `reasoning_effort: none`, matching the judge family now used for ROPG data. The `none` setting is not cosmetic: Luna is a reasoning model, so with reasoning enabled the 16-token completion budget is spent on reasoning tokens and the reply comes back empty — which the old parser turned into a silent score of 0.0. (3) `max_workers` 16 → 4; the pool is recreated per question anyway, so the higher value mostly bought endpoint rate-limit errors. (4) Retry with capped exponential backoff (`max_attempts: 3`, 1s → 8s) at **both** call sites, where there was previously none — a rewrite failure dropped the candidate and a judge failure scored it 0.0, i.e. promoted it to the `rejected` arm. Score parsing is now strict (`SCORE_RE.fullmatch` over `[0, 1]`, the same shape `gen_ropg_data.py` uses), so a malformed or empty reply retries instead of masquerading as a real score, and an empty rewrite is a failure rather than a candidate. Exhausting a budget drops that one candidate — not the whole question, unlike the ROPG group-skip. (5) A persona with fewer than two surviving candidates is skipped: with one candidate, `candidates[0]` and `candidates[-1]` are the same string, which is how **10 rows in the old `data/dpo/train.jsonl` came to have `chosen == rejected`** — a zero-gradient term in the DPO objective. Regeneration cost measured before committing: ≈ **$0.44** on the judge side at Luna standard rates ($0.20/M in, $1.20/M out) for 524 questions × 3 personas × 3 temperatures ≈ 4.7k judge calls; the Grok rewriter is billed separately. The existing `data/dpo/*.jsonl` are the `gpt-5.4-nano`, four-temperature arm and should be moved to `data/dpo/old_v2/` (beside the existing `old_v1/`) rather than overwritten when the regeneration runs.
- *DPO query form was still stem-only — now shares one renderer with ROPG.* The ROPG datagen fix that replaced bare stems with the complete rendered question (`Passage:` / `Question:` / `Options:` / `Pairs:` / `Items:`, gold answer and explanation to the judge only) never reached the DPO pipeline, which kept its own `_load_question_stem` returning `q["stem"]`. Measured over the 524 DPO qids: **0 of 524** stem strings match the rendered form — the renderer always emits a `Question:\n` section, so the mismatch was universal, not limited to the 5 passage-bearing and 48 option-bearing questions (stem-only totals 21,065 tokens vs. 30,567 rendered, +45%; passage questions go 42 → 125 tokens mean). This mattered because the rewriter's output is consumed by the stage-1 retriever, whose LoRA was distilled on the rendered form — `data/ropg_kd/train.jsonl` has 1281/1296 groups starting `Question:\n`, 120 containing `Options:`, 15 containing `Passage:` — so the policy was being trained to produce rewrites from an input shape the pipeline never serves, and for the passage questions from a stem that is often uninterpretable alone. Three changes: (1) `QuestionContext`, `load_question`, `render_question_value`, `render_numbered_section` moved out of `gen_ropg_data.py` into **`src/data/questions.py`**, imported by both datagen stages — verified byte-identical over all 524 questions before and after the move, so `data/ropg_kd/` stays reproducible; (2) the DPO judge now receives the gold answer and explanation as reference context (524/524 have an answer, 524/524 have an explanation) exactly as the ROPG judge does, so it scores a rewrite against the material that actually resolves the question instead of guessing — gold stays out of the rewriter prompt and out of the stored `query`, since a rewrite conditioned on the answer would leak it into retrieval; (3) rows gained `format_version: 1` and `question_ref`, and `rl.dpo_train.load_pairs` now refuses any other version — the shipped `data/dpo/train.jsonl` is correctly rejected by that guard, which is the point: stem-only pairs otherwise load and train without error. Measured cost of the enrichment at Luna rates: +173k judge input tokens ≈ **+$0.035** on top of the $0.44 regeneration, so cost is not a reason to keep the old form. **Still open:** `benchmarks/eval_runner.build_retrieval_query` remains `passage_text + "\n" + stem`, so the reported end-to-end eval path queries the retriever with a third form that matches neither training nor DPO. Deliberately left alone for now because changing it makes previously reported ablation numbers non-comparable and forces a re-run; it should be aligned before the next full eval, and any Stage-1/Stage-2 number reported from that path carries this caveat.
- *DPO datagen format 2 — rubric judge, persisted provenance, labelled cross-persona pairs.* The seed-42 screening found no arm distinguishable from the untrained base, which fires the pre-registered failed-transfer rule in `docs/experiment-design.md:153-156`. The diagnosis was that format-1 pairs carried almost no signal: `chosen` and `rejected` were best-of-3 and worst-of-3 from the *same* Grok model with no margin filter, so the contrast was often style jitter (5.06% of train rows near-duplicate at difflib ≥ 0.90; mean lengths 110.1 vs 107.1 chars), and the labels came from a single bare decimal at `temperature: 1.0` with a 16-token budget, whose self-agreement was 73.0% — the held-out preference accuracy of 0.69 was already at that ceiling. Six changes to `configs/datagen_dpo.yaml` and `src/data/gen_dpo_data.py`. (1) **Rubric judge.** Luna now returns `meaning_preservation` / `persona_fit` / `specificity` under a strict `json_schema` response format, weighted 0.40 / 0.35 / 0.25 into the aggregate that pair selection orders by. Both the prompt block and the schema are generated from `judge.rubric`, and weights must sum to 1.0 or the generator refuses to start. Verified against the live route before adoption: `gpt-5.6-luna` accepts both `temperature: 0` and strict `json_schema`. (2) **Deterministic judging.** `temperature: 1.0 → 0.0`, budget 16 → 200 tokens (a JSON object does not fit in 16), `samples_per_candidate: 1`. Multi-sample averaging is implemented and averages the sub-scores before weighting, but buys nothing at temperature 0 where the replies are near-copies — raise it only alongside `temperature > 0`. (3) **Persisted provenance.** Rows are `format_version: 2` and carry `pair_type`, `chosen_score`, `rejected_score`, `margin`, both sub-score dicts, both temperatures, `rejected_persona_id`, and `judge_samples`; `load_pairs` requires them, and `validate_splits` now reports real score quantiles and pair-type counts instead of two `"unavailable"` strings. Every candidate is additionally dumped to `data/dpo/candidates/` with its `filter_reason`, and the survivors to `data/dpo/candidates_filtered/`, so retuning a threshold means re-running selection over those files rather than paying for generation again — the direct fix for the format-1 defect that forced a full regeneration to answer any question about the score distribution. (4) **Labelled cross-persona pairs.** The old 0.25 gate compared two scores produced under two *different* persona prompts, which is not a like-for-like quantity; each persona's winner is now re-scored under every other persona's rubric context and a row is emitted only when the target persona's own winner beats the foreign rewrite *as judged for that persona*. Costs 6 extra scorings per question. (5) **Real pair filters.** `min_chosen_score: 0.55`, `min_margin: 0.10`, `max_pair_similarity: 0.95`, `cross_persona_min_margin: 0.10`, plus candidate-level `min_judge_samples` and `dedup_candidates`; within-persona negative selection scans upward from the worst survivor instead of taking the extreme, keeping the widest genuine gap when the extreme pair is a cosmetic twin. A startup guard rejects `min_judge_samples > samples_per_candidate`, which would silently empty the dataset after the full run had been paid for. (6) **Four temperatures, `[0.2, 0.5, 0.8, 1.1]`, `max_workers: 8`.** This reverses the format-1 reduction to three on purpose: the point is no longer density inside the model's comfortable band but spread for the judge to rank, and 1.1 sits past that band deliberately. Measured on one question, going 3 → 4 temperatures raised within-persona pair yield from 1/3 to 2/3 personas, and `t=1.1` was the best candidate for one persona and the worst for another. Cost per question is 12 rewrites + 18 judge calls. Two caveats stay open. `min_margin: 0.10` is a guess, not a measurement — the honest calibration is to rescore ~100 candidates twice and take the SD of the difference, which at `samples_per_candidate: 1` needs a separate throwaway pass. And the 0.40/0.35 weighting means fidelity outranks persona fit, so on questions whose wording prescribes a register (e.g. «به زبان ساده») a scholar-appropriate rewrite is penalised on `meaning_preservation` and can lose *for the scholar*; check the `pair_type` × `persona_id` cross-tab after each build for persona skew, since a persona that constantly appears as the negative teaches "don't write like this" far more strongly than the converse. `notebooks/gen_dpo_data.ipynb` still mirrors format 1 and is now stale; its failure mode is safe because `load_pairs` refuses format-1 rows.
- *DPO `filters.min_margin` calibrated — 0.10 was miscalibrated, now 0.04.* Format 2 shipped `min_margin: 0.10` as an explicit guess at the judge's noise floor. `benchmarks/judge_noise.py` measured it over 300 frozen candidates rescored three times, full numbers in [judge-noise-luna-v1](results/judge-noise-luna-v1.md). Three findings changed the config. (1) Luna at `temperature: 0.0` is **not deterministic**: 18.0% of candidates returned byte-identical replies, single-draw aggregate SD 0.0265 (p90 0.041, max 0.137), 0 failures. That falsifies the format-2 rationale for `samples_per_candidate: 1` ("at temperature 0 the replies are near-copies"), which is now **3**, with `min_judge_samples` 1 → 2 so one lost sample does not discard a candidate; the extra cost is ~9.4k → ~28k judge calls, about $6 per full run. (2) `min_margin` was calibrated against the wrong quantity. The script's flip rate scores all six candidate pairs per group, but production emits only the best-versus-worst pair, whose true gap is far wider (mean best-worst margin 0.1288) — so the 24.4% unfiltered flip rate overstates the emitted dataset's error rate, which is ~5% simulated / ~9.5% empirical. Measured on the emitted pair, raising the threshold 0.04 → 0.10 cut surviving groups 62/75 → 34/75 while empirical orientation error moved 7.6% → 7.5%; effective training signal, n(1-2p)^2, peaks at **0.04** on a plateau flat from 0.00. (3) Noise is heteroscedastic — mean SD 0.041-0.043 in the 0.40-0.70 score band against 0.0101 above 0.85 — and per criterion `specificity` (weight 0.25) is the least reproducible at 33% identical across replicates, with `persona_fit` at 39% and `meaning_preservation` at 58%. Pair noise is independent (implied correlation −0.204 ≈ 0), as expected since each candidate is scored in its own call, which licenses the σ√2 scaling. All of this measures judge *self-consistency*, not correctness: the ~30% rubric ambiguity in [judge-agreement-luna-gemini-v1](results/judge-agreement-luna-gemini-v1.md) is a larger ceiling that `min_margin` cannot touch.
- *Open: `cross_persona_min_margin` is still an uncalibrated guess.* It stays at 0.10 and is now deliberately 2.5× stricter than the calibrated within-persona `min_margin: 0.04`, because `benchmarks/judge_noise.py` forms within-persona pairs only and produced no evidence about it. This is not harmless: `cross_persona_min_margin` was the top rejection reason in trial runs, so it throttles exactly the rows that carry the personalization claim (a rewrite scored under its own persona against the same rewrite scored under a different one). Calibrating it needs a variant pass that re-scores each frozen candidate under all three personas and repeats the replicate analysis on those cross-persona deltas. Until then, treat any cross-persona pair-count shortfall as a threshold artifact rather than evidence that personas are indistinguishable.
- *Open: the rewriter temperature ladder buys nothing measurable.* `configs/datagen_dpo.yaml` carries `temperatures: [0.2, 0.5, 0.8, 1.1]`, and 1.1 was included on the rationale that it "widens the quality spread the judge has to rank". Measured over 300 candidates, mean judge score is flat across the ladder (0.828 / 0.824 / 0.811 / 0.825) and each rung wins its group roughly a quarter of the time (18/22/18/17) — indistinguishable from random assignment. What produces the spread is the candidate **count**, as an order statistic: mean best-worst margin 0.1288 at four candidates, 0.1063 at three, 0.0619 at two. Four draws are therefore worth keeping and the values are kept as-is to avoid churning the assets, but the config comment claiming a temperature effect was corrected, and any future budget cut should drop a *judge replicate* before it drops a candidate.
- *Open: `min_chosen_score: 0.55` sits in the noisiest score band.* Judge noise is ~4× larger in the 0.40-0.70 band (mean SD 0.041-0.043) than above 0.85 (0.0101), so the absolute chosen-score bar is set exactly where the judge is least reliable. It barely binds today — only 1 of 75 groups has a best candidate below 0.55, against 5 below 0.60 and 7 below 0.70 — so raising it to 0.70 would cost ~9% of groups to remove the least trustworthy rows. Left at 0.55 because that is a judgement call rather than a measurement; revisit if the regenerated assets show low-scoring groups contributing disproportionately to training instability.
- *Open: only replicate noise was measured, not prompt sensitivity.* The judge-noise design re-runs the identical prompt, so it bounds re-run disagreement and nothing else. A judge can be perfectly reproducible and still swing on cosmetic prompt changes — candidate ordering, a paraphrased persona rendering, or the position of the gold answer in the judge-only context. If prompt-perturbation noise exceeds 0.0265, `min_margin: 0.04` is under-set. Also unmeasured: the `temperature: 1.0` arm the script supports, which would have quantified how much noisier the format-1 label era was; that run used `--judge-temps 0.0` only, so the format-1 → format-2 improvement claim stays qualitative on the noise axis (it remains quantitative on judge self-agreement, 73.0%).
- *Open: the v4 pair data makes `scholar` the loser far more often than the winner.* The as-built audit of the fourth generation, [dpo-data-v4-audit](results/dpo-data-v4-audit.md), confirms the persona-skew risk that the format-2 entry flagged as something to check after each build. Scholar sits on the chosen side of 699 train rows and the rejected side of 1,138 (ratio 0.61, val 0.64), trails both other personas on all three rubric criteria and worst on `meaning_preservation` (0.653 against 0.874 crammer and 0.859 steady), and accounts for 33 of the 40 groups that produced no pair at all. The cross-persona direction counts are more lopsided still: `steady <- scholar` 318 rows against `scholar <- steady` 18, a 17.7x imbalance saying the judge treats "more advanced than needed" as a real failure but "simpler than needed" as tolerable. Mechanically this is the 0.40-weighted fidelity criterion penalising an advanced rewrite of a question whose own wording prescribes a simple register, so it is a property of the rubric weights, not of scholars. Nothing was changed in v4. Two consequences to honour: per-persona eval deltas must be reported separately rather than pooled, because a pooled gain can hide a scholar regression; and if the trained rewriter produces flatter rewrites for scholars than the prompted baseline, this is the first place to look, not a training bug. Fixes in increasing cost: report and move on, re-weight `meaning_preservation` down for scholar, or add a rubric criterion crediting appropriate elaboration.
- *Open: cross-persona rows are 58.8% of the v4 data and are the easier discrimination.* Within-persona pairs contrast two rewrites for the same learner and spread their signal over `meaning_preservation` (+0.191) and `specificity` (+0.176); cross-persona pairs are almost pure `persona_fit` (+0.468, correct sign on 1,564 of 1,564 rows). The majority of the training signal is therefore the register contrast between personas rather than the quality contrast within one, which is exactly the axis a persona-prompted baseline already handles. If Stage-2 eval accuracy comes back high, check it is not carried entirely by the cross-persona subset before claiming the rewriter learned to rank quality; the two subsets are separable by `pair_type`, so the ablation is a filter over the shipped files rather than a regeneration.
